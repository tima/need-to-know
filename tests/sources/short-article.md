# The Pomodoro Technique

Published: March 15, 2018

The Pomodoro Technique is a time management method developed by Francesco Cirillo in the late 1980s. The technique uses a timer to break work into focused intervals, traditionally 25 minutes in length, separated by short breaks. These intervals are known as "pomodoros," named after the tomato-shaped kitchen timer Cirillo used as a university student.

The basic method involves six steps: decide on the task, set a timer for 25 minutes, work on the task until the timer rings, take a short 5-minute break, and after four pomodoros, take a longer 15-30 minute break. The regular breaks help maintain mental freshness and prevent burnout.

The technique has gained popularity among software developers, writers, and students. Its appeal lies in its simplicity and the psychological benefit of breaking large tasks into manageable chunks. The ticking timer creates a sense of urgency that can help overcome procrastination, while the enforced breaks prevent mental fatigue.

Research on the technique shows mixed results. While many users report increased productivity and focus, the rigid 25-minute intervals don't suit all types of work. Creative tasks or complex problem-solving sometimes require longer periods of uninterrupted concentration. The technique works best for tasks with clear, immediate goals rather than open-ended exploration.
