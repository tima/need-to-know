# TL;DR Brief: The Pomodoro Technique
**Source:** The Pomodoro Technique | Published: March 15, 2018

## BLUF
The Pomodoro Technique breaks work into 25-minute focused intervals separated by short breaks, helping maintain mental freshness and overcome procrastination through simple time-boxing.

## Key Points
- Developed by Francesco Cirillo in late 1980s, named after tomato-shaped kitchen timer
- Core method: 25-minute work intervals (pomodoros) + 5-minute breaks, longer break after four pomodoros
- Creates urgency to overcome procrastination while enforcing breaks to prevent burnout
- Popular among software developers, writers, and students for breaking large tasks into manageable chunks
- Works best for clear, immediate goals; less effective for creative tasks requiring extended uninterrupted focus
- Research shows mixed results - rigid 25-minute intervals don't suit all work types

## Takeaways
- Try the technique for task-oriented work with clear deliverables
- Adapt interval length if 25 minutes doesn't match your work rhythm - the principle (focused work + breaks) matters more than exact timing
- Not a universal solution - complex problem-solving may need longer concentration periods
